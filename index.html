02 Scalable Tool Calling

Findings of EMNLP 2025 · Second author

ToolScaler: Scalable Generative Tool Calling via Structure-Aware Semantic Tokenization

Contributed to a generative tool-calling framework that represents roughly 47k tools as composable, structure-aware semantic token sequences. This formulation turns large-tool selection into constrained generation and is designed to scale beyond a fixed retrieval inventory.

Built and quality-checked training data from ToolBench documentation, query-tool pairs, and invocation trajectories. Conducted experiments covering tool retrieval, unseen-tool generalization, and end-to-end invocation, and helped connect the data pipeline, model training, and evaluation into a reproducible research workflow.

03 Graph Anomaly Detection

First author · Manuscript in preparation, target: IEEE TDSC

FGAD: Feedback-Guided Anomalous Diffusion Suppression for Graph Anomaly Detection

Designed a feedback-guided framework to limit the spread of anomalous information through graph neighborhoods. The method estimates anomaly feedback during message passing and suppresses harmful diffusion before it contaminates representations of nearby normal nodes.

Led method design, implementation, experiment construction, and analysis. Against 11 baselines, FGAD achieved the best AUC on 7 of 8 evaluated datasets, including approximately 92% on Inj Flickr. Additional ablations and sensitivity studies were used to isolate the contribution of the feedback and suppression components.

04 Financial Risk Intelligence

National competition project · Team collaboration

ZDC: Intelligent Anti-Money Laundering Detection Platform

Developed a risk-detection prototype for complex financial interactions. The project models entities and transactions as a graph, combines relational patterns with behavioral signals, and organizes suspicious-case discovery into a workflow that supports investigation rather than producing an isolated score.

Contributed to the detection approach, system integration, and presentation of evidence paths for review. The prototype emphasized interpretable graph-based risk cues and a complete analyst-facing workflow. Because source reports contain conflicting evaluation figures, no quantitative performance claim is published here.

05 Service Reliability Research

ICES Research Intern · Research and engineering prototype

Graph Anomaly Detection for Microservice Root-Cause Localization

Explored graph anomaly detection for localizing failures in microservice environments. The prototype converts service dependencies and multi-source operational observations into graph representations, then studies how abnormal nodes and propagation paths can narrow the search space for root causes.

Worked across data preparation, graph construction, model experiments, and prototype integration. The project connected anomaly scoring with dependency-aware diagnosis and practical visualization, while keeping research hypotheses separate from production claims. It is presented as a research and engineering prototype, not a deployed operational system.
